Pour-over coffee is one of the simplest ways to make a clean, bright cup at home. You need only a few things: a dripper, a paper filter, freshly ground coffee, a kettle, and a scale. Start with about fifteen grams of coffee for two hundred and fifty grams of water, ground to the texture of coarse sand. Rinse the paper filter with hot water first; this removes any papery taste and warms the dripper. Heat the water to around ninety-three degrees Celsius, just off the boil. Pour a little water over the grounds and wait thirty seconds. This step, called the bloom, lets carbon dioxide escape so the water can extract flavor evenly. Then pour slowly in small circles, keeping the water level steady, until you reach your target weight. The whole brew should take about three minutes. If the coffee tastes sour, grind finer next time; if it tastes bitter, grind coarser. With a little practice, you will find the balance you like best.
